Cotton Cultivation Guide (કપાસ)

Scientific agronomic management for Bt & non-Bt cotton: Soil preparation, precision drip fertigation, sucking pest thresholds, pink bollworm IPM, and fiber quality preservation.

1. Crop Overview & Agricultural Significance

Cotton (Gossypium hirsutum / Gossypium arboreum), revered as "White Gold," is Gujarat's paramount commercial fiber crop. Gujarat is India's highest cotton producing state, cultivating predominantly in Saurashtra (Surendranagar, Rajkot, Bhavnagar, Amreli), Vadodara, and Bharuch. Successful cultivation requires careful balancing of moisture, vigilant canopy aeration, and preventive pest monitoring.

2. Suitable Climate and Soil Conditions

Soil Type: Thrives best in deep black cotton soils (Vertisols/Regur) with high clay content and high moisture retention, as well as fertile alluvium with good internal drainage. Soil pH should ideally range between 6.5 and 8.0. Waterlogged, saline, or severely compacted shallow soils restrict taproot elongation and trigger early root rot.

Temperature: Requires warm, sunny climate. Minimum 15°C for germination, optimum 21°C–30°C during vegetative elongation, and abundant warm sunshine during boll development and opening. Excessive rains during flowering cause flower drop.

3. Land Preparation & Organic Enrichment

  • Deep Summer Ploughing: Conduct one deep ploughing (25–30 cm) with a mouldboard plough in May to invert soil, destroy hibernating pink bollworm pupae, and expose weed rhizomes to direct sunlight.
  • Harrowing & Clod Crushing: Carry out 2 cross-harrowings followed by planking to create a firm, well-aerated seedbed with fine tilth.
  • Farmyard Manure (FYM): Incorporate 10 to 12 tonnes/hectare of well-decomposed FYM or 5 tonnes/ha vermicompost 3 weeks before sowing to improve water percolation and organic carbon.

4. Sowing Window, Seed Rate & Spacing

Parameter Rainfed (Jirayat) Irrigated / Drip (Bagayat)
Sowing Window After onset of monsoon (Late June to Early July) Pre-monsoon sowing (May 25 to June 15)
Row Spacing 90 cm to 120 cm 120 cm to 150 cm (paired rows for drip)
Plant-to-Plant 45 cm 45 cm to 60 cm
Seed Depth 3 to 4 cm (firm moisture contact, avoid burying deeper than 5 cm)

Seed Treatment: If using non-treated seed, coat with Imidacloprid 70 WS (5g/kg seed) against early sucking pests, followed by Trichoderma viride (10g/kg) and Azotobacter bio-fertilizers.

5. Balanced Nutrient Management (NPK & Micronutrients)

General State Recommendation for Hybrids: 160–240 kg N : 50 kg P₂O₅ : 50 kg K₂O per hectare (subject to Soil Health Card test values).

  • Basal Application: Apply entire Phosphorus (P₂O₅) through Single Super Phosphate (SSP) + 25% Nitrogen + 50% Potash at sowing time placed 5 cm beside seed row.
  • Nitrogen Splits: Apply remaining Nitrogen in 3 equal splits: at squaring (35 DAS), flowering (60 DAS), and peak boll development (80 DAS).
  • Foliar Sprays: Spray 1% 19:19:19 or 2% Potassium Nitrate (13:0:45) at flowering and boll setting to minimize square dropping and improve staple length. Spray 0.5% Zinc Sulfate + 0.2% Boron at 65 DAS.

6. Water Management & Critical Irrigation Stages

Cotton cannot tolerate either chronic water stress or stagnant waterlogging. The 3 most moisture-sensitive stages are:

  1. Square Formation (30–45 DAS): Mild stress tolerated to induce deep rooting; severe drought causes square abortion.
  2. Flowering & Peak Bloom (60–85 DAS): Most critical phase; water shortage drops flower retention by up to 40%.
  3. Boll Development (90–120 DAS): Adequate moisture required for boll expansion and fiber elongation. Stop irrigations when 10–15% bolls begin opening naturally.

7. Integrated Pest & Disease Management (IPM)

Pink Bollworm (PBW) Threshold: Install 5 pheromone traps/ha by 45 DAS. When trap catches exceed 8 moths/night for 3 consecutive days or 10% rosette flowers appear, initiate recommended label sprays immediately.
  • Early Season Sucking Pests (Jassids, Thrips, Aphids): Erect yellow and blue sticky traps (15–20/ha). Spray Neem Oil 10,000 PPM (2 ml/L) as first line of defense. If exceeding ETL, apply Flonicamid 50 WG (0.3g/L) or Diafenthiuron 50 WP.
  • Boll rot & Bacterial Blight: Avoid excessive canopy crowding. Spray Copper Oxychloride 50 WP (2.5g/L) + Streptocycline (0.1g/L) if continuous humid rains induce angular leaf spot.

8. Harvesting & Clean Post-Harvest Handling

  • Pick seed cotton only when bolls are fully opened and morning dew has dried completely (after 10:00 AM).
  • Never mix trash, dried bracts, or immature bolls. Use 100% clean cotton fabric bags (avoid plastic or HDPE bags to prevent synthetic fiber contamination in ginning).
  • Dry seed cotton in shade or on clean tarpaulins until moisture reaches 7–8% before warehouse storage or APMC mandi delivery.

9. Common Farmer Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Excessive early Urea application: Causes vegetative overgrowth, dense shade, and attracts heavy sucking pest attacks.
  • ❌ Prolonging cotton stalks into next summer: Refusing to terminate stalks harbors pink bollworm across seasons. Terminate crop by December–January.
  • ❌ Spraying pesticides at peak noon: High temperatures reduce chemical efficiency and cause rapid leaf scorching. Spray during morning (7–10 AM) or late afternoon.
