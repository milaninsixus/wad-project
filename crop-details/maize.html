Maize Cultivation Guide (મકાઈ / Corn)

High-yielding grain and green fodder management for Eastern Gujarat (Dahod, Panchmahal, Sabarkantha): Hybrid selection, Fall Armyworm (FAW) IPM, and tasseling water management.

1. Overview & Agricultural Role

Maize (Zea mays), known as Makai (મકાઈ), is the primary food and feed crop of the tribal and semi-arid districts of Eastern Gujarat, notably Dahod, Panchmahal, Mahisagar, Aravalli, and Chhota Udepur. It functions as a dual-purpose crop—supplying nutritious grain for local flour (makai no rotlo) and poultry feed industries, while the green stover supplies vital succulent fodder for dairy cattle.

2. Climate, Soil & Varieties

Soil Requirements: Highly sensitive to waterlogging. Prefers deep, fertile, rich, well-drained sandy loam or silt loam soils with neutral pH between 6.5 and 7.5. Stagnant surface water for even 24 hours stunts root respiration and causes leaf chlorosis.

  • Kharif Varieties: Gujarat Maize-6 (GM-6), GM-3, GAYMH-1, HQPM-1 (High Quality Protein Maize rich in Lysine and Tryptophan).
  • Rabi Maize (Winter): Gives exceptional yields (60–75 Q/ha) in irrigated conditions due to low disease pressure and long grain-filling periods.

3. Sowing Window, Spacing & Seed Rate

Season Sowing Window Seed Rate Spacing
Kharif (Monsoon) Onset of monsoon (June 20 to July 10) 18 to 20 kg/ha 60 cm x 20 cm
Rabi (Winter) October 15 to November 15 20 to 22 kg/ha 60 cm x 20 cm

4. Nutrient Management & Nitrogen Splits

Recommended Dose for Hybrids: 120 kg N : 60 kg P₂O₅ : 40 kg K₂O per hectare.

  • Apply full Phosphorus + full Potash + 25% Nitrogen (30 kg) at sowing in furrows.
  • Apply remaining 75% Nitrogen in two equal splits: 50% (60 kg) at knee-high stage (30–35 DAS) and 25% (30 kg) at tasseling/silking stage (50–55 DAS) followed by earthing up.
  • Apply 25 kg/ha Zinc Sulfate basally if soil test indicates deficiency.

5. Critical Water Management Stages

Maize requires 450–600 mm water. The two most critical water stages are Tasseling (pollen shed) and Silking (cob fertilization) around 50–65 DAS. Moisture stress during silking desiccatess silks, causing poor kernel fertilization and half-empty cobs. Provide life-saving irrigation if monsoon halts during this period.

6. Fall Armyworm (FAW) Integrated Pest Management

Fall Armyworm (Spodoptera frugiperda): Aggressive invasive pest. Larvae feed deep inside the leaf whorl, depositing sawdust-like fecal matter and shredding leaves.
  • Seed Treatment: Cyantraniliprole 19.8% + Thiamethoxam 19.8% FS (4 ml/kg seed) grants protection up to 20 days.
  • Push-Pull / Trap Crop: Plant 3–4 border rows of forage sorghum or Napier grass to deflect moths.
  • Whorl Application: Apply sand-sawdust-lime mixture (9:1) or Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt 2g/L) into leaf whorls. If infestation exceeds 10% damage, spray Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC (0.4 ml/L) or Spinetoram 11.7 SC (0.5 ml/L) directing the nozzle directly into leaf whorls.

7. Harvesting & Post-Harvest Storage

Harvest when outer husk leaves turn completely dry, papery white, and a black abscission layer forms at the base of the grain kernel. De-husk cobs, dry in sun for 3–4 days, shell using power maize shellers, and dry clean grains to under 12% moisture for safe storage.
