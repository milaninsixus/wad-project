Castor Cultivation Guide (દિવેલા / Arandi)

Gujarat produces over 80% of India's castor output. Production manual on high-yielding GCH hybrids, wide row spacing, semilooper IPM, and multi-flush harvesting.

1. Overview & Gujarat's Global Leadership

Castor (Ricinus communis), known as Divela (દિવેલા), is an industrial non-edible oilseed of prime export value used in aviation lubricants, pharmaceuticals, and bio-polymers. Gujarat is the world's largest castor production belt, centered across Banaskantha, Sabarkantha, Mehsana, Gandhinagar, and Kutch. Castor's deep taproot system makes it exceptionally resilient to intermittent dry spells.

2. Climate, Soil & Land Preparation

Thrives across diverse soils from sandy loam to medium black soils, provided internal drainage is good. Waterlogged conditions cause rapid wilt. Optimal soil pH is 6.0 to 7.8.

  • Plough deeply (20–25 cm) to break subsoil hardpans for taproot expansion.
  • Incorporate 8–10 tonnes/ha FYM or compost during final tillage.

3. Sowing Window, Hybrids & Spacing

Ideal Sowing Window: 15th July to 15th August. Sowing in late July balances monsoon emergence with optimal autumn flowering temperatures.
  • Proven Gujarat Hybrids: GCH-7 (highly wilt-tolerant, 48% oil content), GCH-8, and GCH-9 (released by SDAU Sardarkrushinagar).
  • Seed Rate: 5.0 kg/ha for hybrids.
  • Wide Spacing: 120 cm to 150 cm row-to-row x 60 cm plant-to-plant. Castor develops extensive lateral branches and demands generous canopy sunlight.

4. Nutrient & Fertilizer Management

Recommended Dose for Irrigated Hybrids: 120 kg N : 50 kg P₂O₅ : 0 kg K₂O per hectare.

  • Apply all Phosphorus + 40 kg Nitrogen basally at sowing.
  • Apply remaining 80 kg Nitrogen top-dressed in 2 equal splits (40 kg each) at 40 DAS (first branch initiation) and 70 DAS (primary spike development) followed by irrigation.

5. Irrigation Schedule

After monsoon withdrawal in September, irrigate at 15–20 day intervals. Critical stages for irrigation are primary spike formation (55–65 DAS), secondary spike development (80–90 DAS), and tertiary spike capsule filling (110–130 DAS). Drip irrigation with inline drippers saves 40% water and enhances branching.

6. Pest & Disease Management

  • Castor Semilooper (Achaea janata): Voracious leaf-feeding looper caterpillar that defoliates plants overnight. Handpick early instars. Spray Bacillus thuringiensis (Bt formulation 1.5 g/L) or Quinalphos 25 EC (2 ml/L).
  • Capsule Borer (Dichocrocis punctiferalis): Larvae web capsules together and bore inside. Spray Profenofos 50 EC (2 ml/L) at capsule emergence.
  • Fusarium Wilt (Fusarium oxysporum f.sp. ricini): Plant certified wilt-resistant hybrids (GCH-7 / GCH-9) and treat seed with Trichoderma harzianum (10 g/kg).

7. Multi-Flush Harvesting

Castor spikes mature sequentially over a 60–90 day span. Harvest individual spikes when 80% capsules on the spike turn yellowish-brown and dry. Do not wait for all tertiary spikes to mature together or primary capsules will shatter. Dry harvested spikes in the sun for 4–5 days and thresh using mechanical castor threshers.

Toxicity Warning: Castor seeds contain the potent natural biological toxin ricin. Never permit livestock or children to chew or ingest castor seeds. Thoroughly wash hands after handling seed stock.
