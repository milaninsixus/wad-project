Wheat Cultivation Guide (ઘઉં)

Comprehensive production manual for irrigated bread wheat and rainfed Bhal durum wheat: Optimal sowing windows, CRI irrigation management, fertilizer efficiency, and rust resistance.

1. Overview & Agricultural Role

Wheat (Triticum aestivum / Triticum durum) is Gujarat's premier Rabi winter cereal. Cultivation spans North Gujarat (Mehsana, Banaskantha, Sabarkantha), Central Gujarat, and the unique rainfed saline tracts of the Bhal region (Ahmedabad and Bhavnagar districts), internationally prized for naturally sweet, lustrous Bhal Daudkhani durum wheat.

2. Climate, Soil & Varieties

Requires cool winter conditions with daily mean temperatures around 20°C–25°C for vegetative tillering and bright sunny days during grain maturation. Warm winters reduce tillering and cause premature forced maturity.

  • Irrigated Bread Wheat Varieties: GW-496, GW-322, GW-366, GW-173 (High yield, 45–55 Q/ha).
  • Bhal Rainfed Durum Varieties: A-206, GW-1, GDW-2 (Grown strictly on conserved soil moisture, prized for high protein and semolina/rava production).

3. Sowing Window, Seed Rate & Row Spacing

Zone / Condition Optimum Sowing Time Seed Rate Row Spacing
Timely Sown Irrigated 15th to 25th November 100–120 kg/ha 22.5 cm
Late Sown Irrigated December 1st to 15th 125–140 kg/ha (increase by 20%) 18–20 cm
Rainfed Bhal Tract Late October to Early November 50–60 kg/ha 30 cm (deep sowing 8–10 cm)
Critical Sowing Depth Rule: In irrigated fields, sow seed strictly at 4 to 5 cm depth. Sowing deeper than 6 cm severely impairs crown root placement and delays tiller emergence.

4. Crown Root Initiation (CRI) & Irrigation Schedule

Irrigated wheat requires 5 to 6 irrigations aligned with physiological milestones:

  1. CRI Stage (20–25 DAS): The absolute most critical irrigation. Crown roots emerge just below the soil surface. Missing this drops yields by 25–35%.
  2. Tillering (40–45 DAS): Determines number of productive grain-bearing tillers.
  3. Late Jointing / Booting (60–65 DAS): Flag leaf expansion and spikelet formation.
  4. Flowering (80–85 DAS): Avoid water stress during pollen shed.
  5. Milking / Soft Dough (100–105 DAS): Plumps grain kernels. Avoid irrigating on windy days to prevent severe plant lodging.

5. Balanced Fertilizer Application

  • Irrigated Dose: 120 kg N : 60 kg P₂O₅ : 40 kg K₂O per hectare.
  • Application Split: 50% Nitrogen + 100% Phosphorus + 100% Potash as basal drill at sowing. Apply remaining 50% Nitrogen top-dressed in 2 equal splits: at 1st irrigation (CRI) and 2nd irrigation (Tillering).
  • Zinc Deficiency Correction: In zinc-deficient sandy soils, apply 25 kg/ha Zinc Sulfate heptahydrate basally, or spray 0.5% ZnSO₄ + 0.25% lime at 35 DAS.

6. Disease & Pest Protection

  • Termites: Treat seed with Chlorpyrifos 20 EC (4 ml/kg seed) before sowing in termite-prone soils.
  • Brown & Yellow Rust: If yellow or brown pustules appear on flag leaves during moist cloudy January weather, spray Propiconazole 25 EC (1 ml/L) or Tebuconazole 25.9 EC immediately.
  • Loose Smut: Treat seed with Carboxin 75 WP (Vitavax) at 2.5 g/kg seed.

7. Harvesting, Threshing & Safe Storage

Harvest when leaves turn straw-yellow, spikes are completely dry, and grain feels hard under thumbnail pressure. After combine or mechanical threshing, sun-dry grain to under 10% moisture. Storing wheat at >12% moisture leads to rapid infesting by grain borers (Rhyzopertha dominica) and weevils.
