Pearl Millet Cultivation Guide (બાજરી / Bajra)

Climate-resilient nutri-cereal for Banaskantha, Kutch & Saurashtra: High-iron GHB hybrids, downy mildew prevention, low-water summer cultivation, and premium fodder handling.

1. Overview & Nutritional Importance

Pearl Millet (Pennisetum glaucum), known locally as Bajri (બાજરી), is India's most drought-tolerant staple cereal. Recognized globally as a "Nutri-Cereal," bajra is naturally gluten-free and packed with essential micronutrients (iron, zinc, dietary fiber, and calcium). It serves as both human food security and indispensable dry fodder (karbi) for Gujarat's extensive dairy herds in Banaskantha and Kutch.

2. Climate, Soil & Seasons

Grown in both Kharif (Monsoon) and Summer (Zaid) seasons in Gujarat. It requires only 250 to 350 mm of water throughout its lifecycle and tolerates air temperatures exceeding 42°C during vegetative growth.

  • Soil: Performs well in light sandy loam, coarse sandy, and alluvium soils with low organic matter where most other cereals fail. Avoid heavy waterlogged clays.
  • Summer Bajra Advantage: In North Gujarat and Bhavnagar, summer bajra sown in February yields 35–45 Q/ha with virtually zero ergot or downy mildew due to dry air.

3. Sowing Window, Seed Rate & Spacing

Season Sowing Window Seed Rate Spacing
Kharif (Monsoon) June 25 to July 15 3.75 to 4.0 kg/ha 45 cm x 10–12 cm
Summer (Zaid) February 15 to March 10 4.0 to 5.0 kg/ha 45 cm x 10 cm

High-Yielding Hybrids: GHB-558, GHB-744, GHB-732, GHB-538, and bio-fortified iron-rich hybrids like HHB-67 Improved.

4. Nutrient Management & Thinning Practice

  • Recommended Dose: 80 kg N : 40 kg P₂O₅ : 0 kg K₂O per hectare for irrigated hybrids (40:20:0 for rainfed).
  • Apply all Phosphorus + 50% Nitrogen basally. Top-dress remaining Nitrogen at 30 DAS following weeding.
  • Mandatory Thinning (Gap Filling): At 15–20 DAS, thin out excess crowded seedlings to maintain exactly 10–12 cm plant spacing. Overcrowded bajra produces thin, sterile tillers.

5. Pest & Disease Management

  • Downy Mildew / Green Ear Disease (Sclerospora graminicola): Leaf chlorosis followed by floral parts transforming into green leafy structures. Treat seed with Metalaxyl 35 SD (Apron 6g/kg) and plant resistant GHB hybrids.
  • Ergot (Claviceps fusiformis): Pinkish honey-like droplets ooze from infected spikelets, turning into black sclerotial bodies. Soak seed in 20% common salt solution (2 kg salt in 10 L water)—light ergot sclerotia float to surface and must be skimmed off and destroyed before planting.
  • Stem Borer: Apply Cartap Hydrochloride 4G in leaf whorls at 30 DAS if dead hearts appear.

6. Harvesting & Grain Curing

Maturity takes only 75–85 days. Cut earheads when grains are hard and dry in the field for 4–5 days before mechanical threshing. Dry cleaned grain down to 10–11% moisture. Stalks (karbi) should be cut immediately after earhead harvest while still green and stacked upright in shocks to preserve succulent livestock forage quality.
