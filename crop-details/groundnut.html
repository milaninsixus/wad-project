Groundnut Cultivation Guide (મગફળી)

Agronomic package for high pod and oil yield: Soil preparation, Rhizobium inoculation, gypsum timing, white grub prevention, and Tikka leaf spot management in Saurashtra.

1. Crop Overview & Regional Economic Importance

Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea), known locally as Magfali (મગફળી), is the premier oilseed crop of Gujarat, with Saurashtra (Rajkot, Junagadh, Jamnagar, Gir Somnath, Amreli) contributing over 70% of the state's production. Groundnut enriches soil by fixing atmospheric nitrogen (up to 40–60 kg N/ha) and provides premium edible oil and high-protein livestock feed (oilcake).

2. Climate, Soil & Land Preparation

Soil Requirements: Prefers well-drained, loose, friable sandy loam, red loamy, or light alluvial soils rich in calcium and organic matter. Heavy clay soils should be avoided because hard crusting restricts peg (gynophore) entry and causes heavy pod breakage during digging. Ideal pH is 6.0 to 7.5.

  • Plough field 15–20 cm deep, followed by 2 harrowings and planking to establish a pulverized, crumbly seedbed free of hard clods.
  • Incorporate 8–10 tonnes/ha well-rotted cow dung manure or compost 2 weeks prior to sowing.

3. Sowing Window, Seed Rate & Spacing

Type Prominent Gujarat Varieties Spacing Seed Rate (Kernels)
Bunch Type (Erect) GG-2, GG-5, GG-7, TG-37A, GJG-9 30 cm x 10 cm 100–120 kg/ha
Semi-Spreading Type GG-20, GJG-22, GG-11 45 cm x 10 cm 80–100 kg/ha
Mandatory Seed Treatment Sequence (FIR):
Fungicide: Mancozeb (2g) or Thiram (3g/kg) against seed rot.
Insecticide: Imidacloprid 600 FS (6.5 ml/kg) in white grub endemic areas.
Rhizobium: Inoculate with Rhizobium and PSB liquid culture (25 ml/10 kg seed) to stimulate nodulation.

4. Nutrient Management & The Crucial Gypsum Stage

Recommended Fertilizer Dose: 12.5–25 kg N : 25–50 kg P₂O₅ : 0–25 kg K₂O/ha based on soil tests.

  • Single Super Phosphate (SSP): Always prefer SSP over DAP because SSP provides 12% vital sulfur needed for oil synthesis.
  • Gypsum Application (Non-Negotiable): Broadcast 250 to 400 kg/ha of Gypsum at 30–35 DAS (pegging phase) around rootzones and lightly earth up. Gypsum delivers soluble calcium directly absorbed by young pegs to prevent "pops" (empty hollow pods without kernels).

5. Irrigation Schedule & Moisture Critical Periods

In Kharif, groundnut is mostly rainfed, but dry spells at critical growth stages require life-saving irrigations:

  1. Flowering (20–25 DAS): Mild moisture needed for synchronous blooming.
  2. Peg Penetration (35–45 DAS): Most critical; dry, crusty soil prevents pegs from entering soil.
  3. Pod Development (60–75 DAS): Water deficit causes shriveled, lightweight kernels.

6. Pest & Disease Protection (IPM)

  • White Grub (Holotrichia consanguinea): Devastating root-cutting pest in sandy tracts. Apply Beauveria bassiana (5 kg/ha in 250 kg FYM) or Chlorpyrifos 20 EC (4 L/ha) through irrigation water if outbreak occurs.
  • Tikka Leaf Spot (Cercospora): Small dark brown spots surrounded by a bright yellow halo. Spray Mancozeb 75 WP (2.5 g/L) or Tebuconazole 25.9 EC (1 ml/L) at first symptom notice.
  • Collar Rot / Stem Rot (Sclerotium rolfsii): Soil drenching with Trichoderma harzianum at 30 DAS.

7. Harvesting, Drying & Aflatoxin Prevention

Maturity Signs: Foliage turns yellowish, inner pod shell turns dark brown or black, and kernels separate cleanly from pod walls.

  • Dig plants when soil has moderate workable moisture.
  • Invert plants in field for 2–3 days to dry pods exposed to sunlight, avoiding pod-soil contact.
  • Thresh and sun-dry pods until moisture drops below 8–9%. Storing wet pods produces toxic Aspergillus flavus (Aflatoxin) which ruins export value.
