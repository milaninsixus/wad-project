Plant Disease & Pest Identification Library

Visual symptom diagnosis, biological distinguishing features, environmental triggers, economic injury levels, and integrated pest management (IPM) protocols.

Diagnostic Disclaimer: Photographs and symptom descriptions are educational diagnostic aids. Nutrient deficiencies, fungal root rots, and viral complexes frequently present overlapping visual symptoms. A definitive diagnosis requires laboratory pathology analysis or field verification by a qualified Agricultural Extension Officer or KVK specialist.
Major Insect Pest Cotton, Okra

Pink Bollworm (Pectinophora gossypiella) & American Bollworm

Symptoms: Small entrance holes in squares and developing green bolls plugged with frass. Flowers fail to open normally, displaying typical twisted "rosette flowers." Young developing bolls drop prematurely; surviving bolls contain stained, damaged lint with hollowed seeds.

How to Distinguish from Similar Conditions: Unlike sucking pest shedding where squares drop cleanly, bollworm damage exhibits brown internal boring channels, chewed staminal columns, and pinkish larvae inside split bolls.
Contributing Weather: Warm, cloudy, humid late-monsoon conditions (25–32°C).
Economic Threshold Level (ETL): 8 moths/trap/night for 3 consecutive days or 10% rosette flowers.
IPM Protocol: Install 5 Pheromone traps/ha at 45 DAS • Release Trichogramma bactrae egg parasitoids (1.5 lakh/ha) • Spray Neem Oil (2 ml/L) • Chemical option if exceeding ETL: Chlorantraniliprole 18.5 SC (0.3 ml/L).

Sucking Pest Cumin, Mustard, Cotton, Vegetables

Aphids (મોલો-મશી / Aphis gossypii & Hyadaphis coriandri)

Symptoms: Large clusters of tiny soft-bodied green, yellow, or black insects feeding on young tender foliage, buds, and inflorescences. Leaves curl downward, cup, and wither. Secretion of sticky "honeydew" covers foliage, attracting black sooty mold that halts photosynthesis.

Distinguishing Feature: Distinguished from thrips and jassids by stationary dense colony clustering under leaves and the conspicuous presence of black sooty fungal crusts and visiting black ants.
Contributing Weather: Overcast cloudy winter weather with mild temperatures (18–24°C) and high humidity.
ETL: 15–20% infested shoots in cumin or 10% leaf curling in cotton.
IPM Protocol: Erect 15 Yellow Sticky Traps/ha • Conserve ladybird beetles and lacewings • Spray Neem formulation 10,000 PPM (2 ml/L) • Chemical option: Imidacloprid 17.8 SL (0.4 ml/L) or Flonicamid 50 WG (0.3 g/L).

Sucking Pest & Viral Vector Cotton, Chilli, Tomato, Pulses

Whiteflies (સફેદ માખી / Bemisia tabaci)

Symptoms: Tiny white moth-like insects that flutter in clouds when the canopy is gently shaken. Leaves turn chlorotic, curl upwards, and develop necrotic margins. Functions as the primary vector for the devastating Cotton Leaf Curl Virus (CLCuV).

Distinguishing Feature: High flight activity upon canopy disturbance distinguishes whiteflies from stationary aphid and scale infestations.
IPM Protocol: Avoid excessive urea • Install yellow sticky traps • Spray Pyriproxyfen 10% EC (1.5 ml/L) for nymph stages, or Diafenthiuron 50 WP (1g/L).

Fungal Disease Cumin, Coriander, Mustard, Vegetables

Powdery Mildew (છારો રોગ / Erysiphe polygoni)

Symptoms: White, talcum powder-like superficial fungal mycelium patches emerge initially on lower leaves, spreading rapidly to stems, branches, and flowering umbels. Infected flowers dry up without setting seeds; early seeds become shriveled, blackened, and unmarketable.

Differential Diagnosis: Unlike Downy Mildew which produces yellow angular lesions on upper leaf surfaces with violet downy growth restricted underneath, Powdery Mildew produces dry white powdery growth visible on both leaf surfaces and stems.
Preventive Protocol: Prophylactic dusting of 300-mesh Sulfur powder (25 kg/ha) at 45 DAS • Foliar spray of Wettable Sulfur 80 WP (3g/L) • In established infections: Azoxystrobin + Difenoconazole (1 ml/L).

Fungal Disease Groundnut (Tikka Disease)

Tikka Leaf Spot (Cercospora arachidicola & C. personata)

Symptoms: Early leaf spot: circular reddish-brown spots with prominent bright yellow halos appearing 3–4 weeks after emergence. Late leaf spot: smaller, darker black circular spots mostly lacking yellow halos. Causes severe premature defoliation, starving developing underground pods.

Distinguishing Feature: Distinguished from bacterial spots by the regular circular necrotic centers and powdery conidial sporulation on the underside of spots.
Control Protocol: Seed treatment with Thiram (3g/kg) • Spray Mancozeb 75 WP (2.5 g/L) at first symptom appearance • Follow with systemic Tebuconazole 25.9 EC (1 ml/L) if disease persists.

Soil-Borne Fungal Complex Cotton, Castor, Cumin, Pulses

Root Rot & Vascular Wilt Complex (Fusarium / Rhizoctonia / Macrophomina)

Symptoms: Sudden drooping and loss of turgidity in leaves during sunny afternoons while soil moisture is ample. Leaves turn yellow, dry, and remain attached to dead stalks. Uprooting shows shredding of taproot, dark brown vascular discoloration when stems are split longitudinally, and bark peeling off the collar region.

Distinguishing Feature: Splitting the lower stem reveals dark brown to black internal vascular ring staining. Drought-stressed plants show no vascular browning and recover rapidly overnight if watered.
IPM Protocol: Crop rotation with sorghum/pearl millet • Biological soil application: Trichoderma viride (5 kg/ha mixed in 500 kg FYM) • Seed coating with Carbendazim (2g/kg) • Improve field drainage.

Physiological Deficiency All Crops (Paddy, Cotton, Wheat, Maize)

Key Crop Nutrient Deficiencies (Zinc, Nitrogen, Iron)

Zinc (Zn) Deficiency:
Interveinal chlorosis on middle leaves, bleached white bands in maize ("white bud"), rusty bronze speckling in paddy ("Khaira disease").
Nitrogen (N) Deficiency:
Uniform pale yellowing starting from oldest lower leaves upward in a distinct V-shaped pattern along leaf midribs. Stunted stems.
Distinguishing Feature: Nutrient deficiencies appear symmetrically across broad rows without fungal sporulation, bacterial slime, or chewing holes.
Correction: Basal application of 25 kg/ha Zinc Sulfate • Foliar spray of 0.5% ZnSO₄ + 0.25% lime • Top-dress Urea according to Soil Health Card parameters.
