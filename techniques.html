Micro-Irrigation

Drip Irrigation & Venturi Fertigation System Management

Application of water directly to the plant root zone drop-by-drop at low pressure through an engineered network of valves, filters, mainlines, sub-mains, and lateral drippers.

Primary Advantages

  • Saves 40% to 60% irrigation water compared to traditional furrow or flood methods.
  • Fertigation: Soluble fertilizers (19:19:19, Urea, Potassium Nitrate) delivered directly into root zones, improving fertilizer use efficiency by 30–40%.
  • Reduces weed proliferation between rows since inter-row soil remains dry.
  • Prevents fungal foliar diseases by keeping leaves dry.

Operational Limitations

  • Initial installation capital investment (partially offset by Gujarat Green Revolution Company - GGRC subsidies).
  • Emitter clogging from high carbonate, bicarbonate, or iron content in borewell water.
  • Damage to thin lateral pipes by rodents or mechanical weeders.

Step-by-Step Maintenance Protocol: Acid Treatment for Hard Water

In areas with high TDS groundwater (Saurashtra and North Gujarat), calcium carbonate scales gradually plug drippers. Treat with Hydrochloric Acid (HCl 33%): Inject acid through the venturi until water pH at lateral tail ends reaches 4.0. Close system for 12–24 hours, then thoroughly flush all sub-mains and lateral flush valves with fresh water.

State Subsidy Linkage: Gujarat farmers can apply for 70% to 85% financial assistance on micro-irrigation systems through the Gujarat Green Revolution Company (GGRC) portal (ggrc.co.in).

Soil Health Management

Scientific Soil Testing & Soil Health Card Interpretation

Routine laboratory soil analysis measures pH, electrical conductivity (salinity), organic carbon, available N, P, K, and essential micronutrients (Zn, Fe, Cu, Mn, B).

Step-by-Step Field Soil Sampling Method (The "V-Shape" Protocol)

  1. Select Sampling Grid: Divide the farm into uniform parcels based on slope, soil color, and cropping history. Collect 15–20 sub-samples in a zig-zag pattern across 1 hectare.
  2. Clear Surface: Remove surface weeds, dried leaves, and stones without removing topsoil.
  3. Make a V-Shaped Cut: Using a spade or khurpi, dig a V-shaped pit to a depth of 15 cm (6 inches) for field crops (or 30–60 cm for orchards).
  4. Slice a Core: Shave a uniform 2–3 cm thick vertical slice of soil from the face of the pit from top to bottom.
  5. Quartering Method: Mix all sub-samples thoroughly in a clean plastic bucket. Spread in a circle on clean cloth, divide into 4 quarters, discard two opposite quarters, and repeat until approximately 500 grams of composite soil remains.
  6. Label & Dispatch: Dry in shade (never direct sun or oven). Pack in a clean polythene bag with farmer name, survey number, and previous crop, then submit to your nearest KVK or Government Soil Testing Lab.

Agronomic Diversification

Legume Crop Rotation & Intercropping Models

Systematic sequential cropping and simultaneous companion planting to break continuous pest cycles, reduce chemical fertilizer dependency, and maximize farm income per square meter.

Proven 2-Year Rotation Models (Gujarat)

  • Model A (Saurashtra): Groundnut (Kharif) → Wheat or Cumin (Rabi) → Green Gram/Moong (Summer).
  • Model B (Central Gujarat): Bt Cotton (Kharif) → Summer Pearl Millet/Bajra → Pigeon Pea/Arhar (Next Kharif).
  • Model C (North Gujarat): Castor (Kharif) → Cumin or Mustard (Rabi) → Fodder Cowpea.

Intercropping Row Ratios

  • Cotton + Green Gram / Black Gram: 1:1 or 1:2 ratio. Short duration pulse fixes nitrogen and is harvested before cotton canopy closes.
  • Castor + Groundnut: 1 row of castor every 3–4 rows of groundnut. Maximizes early groundnut returns without shading.
  • Pearl Millet + Moth Bean / Cowpea: 2:1 ratio for drought-prone Banaskantha tracts.

Biological Recycling

On-Farm Vermicompost Production (Eisenia fetida)

Conversion of crop residues, cotton stalks, and cow dung into biologically active, odorless, dark granular vermicompost within 50 to 60 days.

Bed Construction & Feeding Protocol

  1. Location & Shading: Select elevated, shaded ground sheltered from direct sunlight and torrential rains under a thatch or agro-net roof.
  2. Bed Dimensions: Standard permanent bed size: 10 feet length x 3 feet width x 2 feet height constructed with bricks or HDPE vermibeds.
  3. Base Drainage Layer (3–4 inches): Layer crushed crop residues, dry twigs, and coconut coir to allow aeration and drainage.
  4. Semi-Decomposed Feed Layer (1.5 feet): Mix 60% chopped farm biomass with 40% cattle dung pre-rotted for 10–15 days to release initial fermentation heat. (Fresh hot dung will kill earthworms).
  5. Earthworm Inoculation: Introduce 1 kg (approx. 1,000 worms) of red wiggler earthworms (Eisenia fetida) per 10 feet bed on the surface.
  6. Moisture & Gunny Bag Cover: Maintain 60% moisture content by sprinkling water regularly. Cover bed with moist gunny bags to maintain darkness.
  7. Harvesting: After 45–60 days, loose black granular tea-leaf-like worm casts accumulate on the surface. Stop watering 3 days before harvesting to drive worms to the bottom, then scrape off top castings and sieve through a 3 mm screen.
